`src/GstTaxReports.jsx` and `src/TdsReports.jsx` are the two pages of the Tax & Compliance section, and `src/tax-compliance.js` is the single pure projection module behind both. The sidebar group lives inside `Reports` as `Tax & Compliance` and holds exactly two leaves - `GST / Tax Reports` (route name `GST Reports`) and `TDS Reports` - so the shape is `Tax & Compliance` -> GST / Tax Reports, TDS Reports, and nothing else. Both pages are mounted through `createPortal` from `src/Navigation.jsx` into the shared `.transactionPortal`, which is why `src/App.jsx` excludes the two route names from the operational-module switch, and `src/tax-compliance.css` styles both. Read this document before changing either page or the projection.

The section is accounting-level reporting and prepares NO statutory return. On 2026-09-29 the two return projections were DELETED, not merely unlinked: `gstr1Groups` (outward-supply groups) and `gstr3bSummary` (the summary) were removed from `src/tax-compliance.js`, their tabs, sheets, tables and cards were removed from `src/GstTaxReports.jsx`, the return-only rules `.tcEmphasis td` and `.tcSub` were removed from `src/tax-compliance.css`, the return example was removed from the dashboard content in `src/App.jsx`, and every `GSTR` token was purged from `src/`. That removal is an invariant: this page never files, prepares, validates or reformats a return, and the words static return, statutory return and GSTR belong nowhere in this section. If a future brief asks for a return, it is a new module with its own filing contract - not a tab here.

`GST / Tax Reports` opens on `<h1>GST / Tax Reports</h1>` and the subtitle `View GST collected, paid and tax positions for your business.` under the shared report heading and toolbar: a 300px search beside one `Filters` disclosure that counts its active filters, then `Export Excel` and `Export PDF`. The disclosure owns Date from, Date to, Transaction type, Branch, Customer / Supplier, GSTIN, Tax rate and Tax type, plus `Refresh` and `Clear filters`. The date range is INCLUSIVE at both ends (`row.date < from` and `row.date > to` are the only exclusions); Branch merges the accessible organisations' branches with the branches the rows carry; and every other option is derived from the rows themselves through `gstOptions`, so no filter can offer a value no document holds. Clear filters resets the filters, the search and the page together.

The four summary cards are `Taxable Sales`, `Output GST`, `Input GST` and `Net GST Position`, and they follow the filtered rows; `Net GST Position` carries the caption `Output GST less input GST`. `summarizeGst` sums the row components - never a second recalculation - and the cards are stated above the tabs so the position reads the same from any tab.

The tabs are exactly four, in this order: `GST Summary` (the `useState` default), `Input GST`, `Output GST` and `HSN / SAC Summary`. Each carries its own plain note rather than a figure no data model supports: the summary states `Overview of GST recorded on sales and purchases.`, Input GST states `GST recorded on purchases. This prototype does not track input tax credit status, so no eligibility is claimed.`, Output GST states `GST charged on sales.`, and HSN / SAC states `GST summary grouped by item or service classification.` Three tabs are one list each - the whole filtered list, its `direction === 'output'` slice and its `direction === 'input'` slice - and the fourth is an aggregate. `GST Summary` must stay first and default.

The register table is ten columns on the summary (Date, Transaction Type, Document No., Customer / Supplier, GSTIN, Taxable Amount, CGST, SGST, IGST, Total GST) and nine on the two direction tabs, whose second and third columns rename themselves to `Invoice / Document No.` with `Customer` and `Bill / Document No.` with `Supplier`. The Document No. is a link-styled `button.tcLink` that opens the owning document through the same `sessionStorage` handshake the Transaction Register uses, the caption reads `N of M GST documents`, and a `tfoot` states the totals of the rows in view. `GST Summary`, `Output GST` and `Input GST` are paged (`PAGED`), twenty-five rows at a time by default with 25 / 50 / 100 offered through the shared `.pagination-footer` pager reading `Showing A-B of N transactions`; the HSN / SAC tab is a grouped aggregate with its own `tfoot` and is never paged.

`HSN / SAC Summary` groups the OUTWARD lines by their HSN / SAC code through `hsnSummary` and states the description, quantity, taxable amount, CGST, SGST, IGST and total GST per code, with a total row. An outward line with no code is stated under `Not specified` rather than dropped. The view covers outward supplies only and its caption says so - `HSN / SAC Summary · N codes from outward supplies` - because an inward bill line carries one combined tax amount and no per-component split, so it cannot be attributed to a code honestly. Keep that limitation stated rather than softened.

The page keeps the report family's states and its reconciliation. `gstReconciliation` compares the rows in view with the GST control accounts the ledger actually posted and prints `GST balances reconcile with the ledger accounts.` when the difference is zero, or the difference with `documents state X against Y posted to the GST accounts` when it is not - it states the difference rather than forcing it away. A cancelled document is dropped by the projection before any tab sees it. When the book holds drafts the note appends `Only posted documents are stated: N draft or unposted documents are excluded`, the empty table renders the shared `EmptyState` with different copy for no GST transactions found and no transactions match your filters, and a read failure renders an alert with `Retry`.

Exports state exactly what the screen states. `Export Excel` writes the active tab's own header and rows through `tableExport` and `excelReport` as `wayvida-gst-<tab>.xml` behind the report identity block, with amounts in RUPEES because every other Wayvida export writes rupees; `Export PDF` is the application's `window.print()` path and prints from the `tcPrintMeta` block. The workbook and the PDF therefore carry the organisation, branch, date range, tab, transaction type, tax type, tax rate, party, GSTIN, search, the row count and the generation stamp.

The GST rows are the posted documents the sales and purchase engines already wrote, collected by `collectGstDocuments` through the same document reader the Credit Notes module uses: posted sales invoices and credit notes (the `output` direction) and posted purchase bills and debit notes (the `input` direction). A credit note states its amounts at -1 and a bill at +1, the total of a row is the sum of its own components, and every money value in the projection is integer PAISE through the shared `money()` helper, so the projection stays comparable with the ledger and only the page formats. An unposted document is excluded and counted in the note; a cancelled document is dropped.

`TDS Reports` reuses the same shell with its own vocabulary. Its cards are `TDS Applicable Base`, `TDS Deducted`, `TDS Paid` and `TDS Payable` - the value of supply TDS was computed on, the withholding the rows imply, the withholding already taken on the documents the ledger has settled, and the remainder; its tabs are exactly three, in this order: `TDS Summary` (the `useState` default), `TDS Deducted` and `TDS Payable`, and all three page twenty-five rows at a time through `PAGED`; and its filters are Date from, Date to, Branch, Vendor / Payee, PAN, TDS Section, TDS Rate and Status (`Deducted` or `Pending`), while the search reads the vendor, the vendor code, the PAN, the document number and the payment reference. The shared `StatusPill` carries the tone and the word is printed beside it, so a state never depends on colour alone. Vendor and section are a GROUPING of the register, never a view of their own: a `Group by` control offering `None`, `Vendor` and `TDS Section` sits inside the Summary report card above its table, and choosing one re-reads the same rows through the shared `tdsByVendor` / `tdsBySection` builders as a heading row followed by that group's documents - the same nine columns, the same rows, the same figures, and no separate page, tab or sidebar entry. A grouped register does not page, so a heading is never split from the rows and totals it heads, and the Excel export keeps the grouping as a leading column rather than as a second report.

Withholding is derived, never guessed. `collectTdsDocuments` reads only a POSTED purchase bill whose vendor carries BOTH a TDS section and a rate, and `tdsOn(value, rate)` rounds the deduction to the rupee and returns zero for a section with no rate; a draft bill, a bill whose vendor has no section and a vendor with no rate all contribute nothing. Each row also carries the vendor's id and code and the document's own reference, so the payee opens the Vendors module through the same `wayvida-open-vendor` handshake the Supplier Outstanding report uses and the document number opens the Purchases module through the existing `wayvida-open-bill` handshake. `TDS Deducted` states the rows the ledger has settled and `TDS Payable` the rows with a balance left, both through the shared `tdsRowBalance(row)`: on a settled document `TDS Paid` is the deduction itself and `Balance Payable` is zero, so a fully paid document never appears in the outstanding table or in its total. This is still a payment-status reading and not a deposit register - this prototype records NO TDS challan or deposit, and the note says so. The bottom reconciliation block names its three figures - `TDS Deducted`, `TDS Paid` and `TDS Payable` - from `tdsReconciliation`, which computes the payable independently as the withholding on the unsettled rows rather than by subtracting, so the stated relationship `TDS Payable = TDS Deducted - TDS Paid` is CHECKED rather than assumed: it prints `✓ TDS balances reconcile` in the ok tone when the difference is zero, and `⚠ TDS balances need review` with `Difference: Rs X` in the warning tone when it is not, and no difference is hidden.

Three model limits are deliberate and stated rather than papered over: there is no input tax credit status model, so the Input GST tab claims no eligibility; an inward bill line carries one combined tax amount, so the HSN / SAC view is outward-only; and the `/ REV` reversal branch in `collectGstDocuments` has no producer anywhere in the app - it is a dead path left intact because deleting it would change no behaviour and would lose the reversal contract the reader was written to honour.

One shell rule bites here and is only visible in the running app: `src/ui-system.css` applies `th,td{padding:16px 20px!important;height:...}` to every table cell, and the sibling reports win with bare element selectors scoped by the report card (`.tcRegisterCard th,.tcRegisterCard td`). Never convert those to class-only selectors - the shared rule would win again.
