# Business Reports

**Last verified:** 2026-09-29

The Business Reports section is the sidebar group `Business Reports` inside `Reports` and holds exactly three leaves: `Sales Report`, `Purchase Report` and `Expense Report`. `src/SalesReport.jsx`, `src/PurchaseReport.jsx` and `src/ExpenseReport.jsx` are the three pages, `src/business-reports.js` is the ONE pure projection module behind all three and `src/business-reports.css` is their one stylesheet; the three pages are mounted through `createPortal` from `src/Navigation.jsx` into the shared `.transactionPortal`, which is why `src/App.jsx` excludes the three route names from the operational-module switch and from the welcome banner. The section answers the three questions a business owner asks about its own trading - what did we sell, what did we buy and what did we spend - and it is read-only: the pages read the stores and pass them in, the projection never posts, edits, reverses or recalculates anything, and no accounting engine is touched.

Every figure is read back from the record the owning engine already wrote, so these are projections and never a second set of books. Sales reads `readAccounts()` (`src/account-store.js`) and states each invoice's own `totals.taxable`, the four tax components (`cgst`, `sgst`, `igst`, `cess`) as one GST figure and the document total; the customer code is joined from the customer master (`wayvida-customers`) because an invoice stores the customer's name and id rather than repeating its code. Purchase reads the same book's `purchaseBills` and joins the supplier code from the vendor master (`wayvida-vendors-v1`). Expense reads `readOperations()` (`src/operations-store.js`, key `wayvida-operations-v1`) and resolves the expense account and the paid-through account to the chart of accounts' own names through `src/account-store.js`. Money is integer paise throughout and is formatted by the pages through the shared `money()`.

One row per document is a guarantee. A sales invoice, a purchase bill and an expense each appear exactly ONCE, with the amount their own document carries; debit and credit lines belong to the Day Book, the General Ledger and the Journal register, and the receivable and payable balances belong to Customer Outstanding and Supplier Outstanding, so none of them is repeated here. Neither a credit note nor a debit note is folded into a business report: a credit note belongs to the sales ledger and the GST return, and reading one into a sales total would double-count a supply the invoice already stated. No report states a receivable, a payable, an outstanding balance or a payment status.

The status vocabulary is the three lifecycle words a business report can explain - `Draft`, `Pending` and `Posted` - reduced by `lifecycle(status, posted)` from the document's stored status plus its own module's posting rule, which is the same reduction `src/transaction-register.js` performs so the two reports can never disagree. A cancelled, voided, excluded or reversed document is dead and is excluded from the report entirely, so a cancelled invoice can never reach a sales total; a purchase bill's stored status is a PAYMENT status (`Unpaid`, `Partially Paid`, `Paid`), so its `posted` flag decides its lifecycle and the payment word is never printed as a status. The default Status filter is `Posted`, which is the accounting view a business report is read for, and `All` is an explicit option that states the drafts too; the caption says which view is on screen. A row that cannot both hold and state its document is impossible: the projection's `checks` re-derive the identity `Total = Taxable + GST + Round Off` (Expense: `Total = the recorded amounts`), the one-row-per-document guarantee, the posted scope, the non-negative amounts and the date range, and the page prints them rather than assuming them.

A document belongs to the organisation that raised it. A row whose organisation is non-empty and different from the working organisation (or its code) is counted as excluded rather than shown, so another organisation's invoice can never enter a total. The branch is the branch the working context set when the document was raised and, when the document does not carry one, the branch of the journal it posted - exactly the rule Customer Outstanding and the Transaction Register already apply. The stored value is then resolved to the organisation's own branch NAME through `branchName(value, branches)`, so the Branch filter offers `Kochi` rather than the internal `abc-kochi`, and a value that names no branch is stated as it was stored rather than dropped.

The Expense tax question is answered honestly rather than guessed. The expense record stores a tax LABEL (for example `GST 18%`) and one amount - the amount that was actually spent - and the expense posting debits the expense account and credits the payment account for that whole amount with no separate input tax line, so the model does not know how much of an expense was tax. The Expense Report therefore states the recorded amount in its Amount column, the recorded RATE LABEL in its Tax column, and NO rupee tax figure anywhere; the Tax card reads `Recorded as a rate` with its tooltip and the page carries a note that says why. `totals.taxable` and `totals.gst` are both zero for an expense by design, and the module header, the page and this document all state the limitation.

The three pages reuse the report family's shell rather than growing their own. The heading is one `<header>` carrying a 300px search beside the Filters disclosure, `Refresh`, `Clear filters` and ONE `Export` button whose dropdown holds `Export Excel` and `Export PDF`; the disclosure owns Date From, Date To and the report's own narrowing controls, and both date bounds are inclusive at both ends. Every option a filter can offer is derived from the rows themselves, so a filter never names a state or a party no document carries. Each report states four cards - Sales: Total Sales, Taxable Sales, GST, Invoices; Purchase: Total Purchases, Taxable Purchases, GST, Bills; Expense: Total Expenses, Tax, Number of Expenses, Paid from Bank - computed from the FILTERED rows, so a card always describes exactly the table below it. The columns are the brief's: Sales and Purchase each state Date, Invoice / Bill, Customer / Supplier, Taxable Amount, GST, Total, Status and Actions, and Expense states Date, Expense No., Expense Account, Payee, Category, Amount, Tax, Total, Status and Actions. The register pages twenty-five rows at a time and offers 25/50/100 through the shared `.pagination-footer` pager, the caption reads `Showing 3 of 4 sales invoices - posted invoices`, and Sales and Purchase keep a grand-total `<tfoot>` row that restates their cards. The shared `StatusPill`, `EmptyState` and `OutstandingActions` row menu are reused rather than reimplemented, and the pages refresh on the `wayvida-accounts-updated` event, on `storage` and on the explicit Refresh action.

Row actions hand off to the workflow that already owns the document, through the existing sessionStorage handshakes: Sales opens Invoices through `wayvida-open-invoice` and the customer statement through `wayvida-credit-customer`; Purchase opens Purchase Bills through `wayvida-open-bill` and the Vendors module through `wayvida-open-vendor`; Expense opens Expense Claims through `wayvida-open-expense`. Export Excel writes the visible rows through the report's own sheet builder (`salesExportSheet`, `purchaseExportSheet`, `expenseExportSheet`) plus the shared `tableExport`, `excelReport` and `downloadReport` helpers, so every export carries the same organisation, branch, period, applied-filter and generated-by identity block the other reports write; Export PDF is the application's print path. Both are disabled when nothing is visible, and an unfiltered control is never listed as an applied filter.

The ordering is the Transaction Register's own control, re-exported rather than redefined: `ALL`, `DEFAULT_SORT`, `SORT_OPTIONS` and `sortTransactions` come from `src/transaction-register.js`, so `Newest first` is the default and there is one definition of it in the product. The stylesheet declares only `br*` classes and deliberately does NOT redeclare `.transactionPortal`, which stays owned by `src/transaction-register.css`; its print block neutralises the shell chrome for all three pages through `body:has(.brPage)` and `.transactionPortal:has(.brPage)`, and its column widths, sticky balance strip, pager and `max-width` 1100px / 900px / 760px / 560px breakpoints follow the family.

Deliberate limitations, stated rather than hidden:

- The Expense Report cannot state a rupee tax figure, because the expense record stores one amount and a rate label and no separate tax component. See `docs/KNOWN-GAPS-AND-ROADMAP.md`.
- The optional Salesperson, Item and Item Category filters the brief allows are not offered on Sales or Purchase, because no invoice, bill or line record carries a salesperson, an item id or an item category that a projection could filter on without inventing one.
- The Expense Report renders its Branch filter only when an expense actually carries a branch (read from the posted journal), because a branch filter that could only ever empty the table would be a control with no honest use.
- The production build cannot run in this sandbox (`npx vite` fails with `esbuild spawn EPERM` while loading `vite.config.mjs`, identically at `HEAD`), so validation is the preview server plus the render harness.

Do not merge, rename or delete `Sales Report`, `Purchase Report` or `Expense Report` in favour of the older `Purchase Reports` page (`src/PurchaseReports.jsx`): `Purchase Reports` is a different, pre-existing module and the two differ only by their trailing `s`.
