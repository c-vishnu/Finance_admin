# Trial Balance

**Last verified:** 2026-09-28

> **Report toolbar update — 2026-09-29:** the heading action row now carries a date-range preset dropdown (`Today`, `This week`, `This month`, `This year`, `Custom selection`; `All dates` is deliberately absent on a report read as of a single date, and a preset writes the CLOSE of its period) beside the search box and the `Filters` disclosure, and ONE `Export` button whose dropdown holds the report own exports. Where a sentence below still describes `Export Excel` and `Export PDF` as two side-by-side buttons, the dropdown supersedes it. See `docs/REPORT-TOOLBAR.md`.

`src/TrialBalance.jsx` is a read-only, date-based statement of every ledger account's debit and credit balance, and `src/trial-balance.js` is its pure projection. `trialBalance(state,{date,branch,account,accountType,search,includeZero})` walks the shared account store, keeps only journals that are posted (`!journal.status||journal.status==='Posted'`) and dated on or before the As-of date, and nets each account's posted movements (`net=debit-credit`). Every account is therefore stated on one side only - `debitBalance` or `creditBalance` - so the same account can never appear in both columns, and the report cannot be forced to balance. Drafts never reach it: an unposted document is not an accounting transaction and carries no journal lines, so there is nothing to project. Cancelled source documents are outside the posted journal, while a posted reversal is included as an entry in its own right, which is what makes a reversed pair net to zero.

`ACCOUNT_TYPE_LABELS`, `TRIAL_BALANCE_TYPES`, `GROUP_ORDER` and the `nature`/`normalSide` fields on each row are presentation metadata only. Normal balance (Assets and Expenses debit; Liabilities, Equity and Income credit) describes which side a balance is expected on; it never assigns a balance. Classification reuses the Chart of Accounts hierarchy through `normalizeAccounts`, `account.group`, `accountGroupFor` and `inferAccountNature` from `src/account-master.js` - there is no second account classification in this module. Group accounts (`isGroup`) and accounts outside the five reportable types are excluded, and zero-balance accounts are hidden unless the Include zero-balance toggle is on; that toggle is a display filter and never touches the account records.

Rows are grouped by account type and then by the account's own group, in `GROUP_ORDER` (for example Assets: Cash and Bank, Current Assets, Fixed Assets, Other Assets), each group and type printing a subtotal, and the statement closing on a grand total. `report.rows` carries the visible accounts and `report.sections` the rendered hierarchy; `debit`, `credit`, `difference` (`debit-credit`), `balanced` and `accounts` describe the current selection, so filtering by account type or account legitimately reports a non-zero difference and the interface says so instead of hiding it. `trialBalanceExportRows(report,context)` is the export shape for the shared CSV/Excel writer in `src/daybook-export.js`: money leaves the engine in integer paise, as every other report export does, and printed rupees. The projection is pure - it never posts, edits, reverses, deletes or creates a journal, account or entry.

The page reuses the report shell already established by the Balance Sheet and the Day Book: one white report surface, a quiet heading with the title and the description "View debit and credit balances for all ledger accounts as of a selected date.", an inline account search, an anchored Filters popover, Export Excel and Export PDF. Four summary cards state Total Debit, Total Credit, Difference and Accounts from the current report, and the statement caption repeats the As-of date. The page deliberately renders no report identity card: the organisation / title / "Posted accounting entries only" / As-of block that the Balance Sheet still shows above its statement was removed on 2026-09-28, because it repeated the heading, printed a demo organisation name on a production accounting statement and pushed the statement a card further down the page. The date and scope it carried are still stated, by the caption, the summary cards and the printed metadata. Do not reintroduce it. The statement table is Account Code, Account, Account Type, Debit and Credit, with section, group, type and grand-total rows, and a sticky green validation footer that reads "Trial Balance balances / Difference: ₹0.00" or, on a genuine mismatch, "Trial Balance does not balance" with the actual difference. An account row is a link into the General Ledger for that account - it writes `sessionStorage['wayvida-open-account']` and navigates to General Ledger, which is the same handoff the Balance Sheet uses. Filters own the As-of date, Branch, Account, Account type and the zero-balance toggle, and account and account-type options are derived from the loaded chart rather than hard-coded. Search matches account code, account name, account type, account nature and group, and is applied through `useDeferredValue` so the page reports `aria-busy` while recalculating instead of freezing. The page reads the account store and never writes it.

Two shell interactions are worth preserving. First, the page heading is a `<header>` element and `src/styles.css` still carries a bare `header{position:fixed;z-index:10;top:0;left:206px;right:0;height:58px;...}` rule with `src/ui-system.css` padding every `th`/`td` with `16px 20px !important`; `.tbHead` therefore restates `position:static!important;inset:auto!important;z-index:auto!important;height:auto!important` exactly as `.bsHead` does, because without it the heading is lifted out of the flow and overlaps the summary cards. Second, `th:last-child,td:last-child{...text-align:right}` right-aligns the section and group heading rows, whose only cell spans the whole row, so `.tbStatementCard .tbSection th,.tbStatementCard .tbGroup th` opts back to `text-align:left!important` and the total rows' label cells do the same through `:not(.tbMoney)`. Both defects were invisible to the bundler and only appeared in the running app.

Below 700px the Account Type column is hidden and below 560px Account Code follows it, so the phone layout keeps Account, Debit and Credit readable instead of forcing a page-level horizontal scrollbar; the statement table itself keeps the same internal card scroll as the Day Book. Printing neutralises the report portal and prints the header metadata, the full hierarchy and the validation line.
