# Customer Outstanding

**Last verified:** 2026-09-28

> **Report toolbar update — 2026-09-29:** the heading action row now carries a date-range preset dropdown (`Today`, `This week`, `This month`, `This year`, `Custom selection`; `All dates` is deliberately absent on a report read as of a single date, and a preset writes the CLOSE of its period) beside the search box and the `Filters` disclosure, and ONE `Export` button whose dropdown holds the report own exports. Where a sentence below still describes `Export Excel` and `Export PDF` as two side-by-side buttons, the dropdown supersedes it. See `docs/REPORT-TOOLBAR.md`.

`src/CustomerOutstanding.jsx` is a read-only, date-based receivables subledger and `src/customer-outstanding.js` is its pure projection. `customerOutstanding(state,customers,{asOf,branch,customer,status,aging,search})` reads the posted accounting records and answers one question - how much does each customer owe us as of the selected date. It is a subledger, not a second General Ledger: it never posts, edits or reverses anything, and it never re-derives a receivable from a UI document while posted accounting data exists.

Outstanding is the invoice's own posted total less the money actually applied to that invoice: the receipt allocations the receipt engine wrote (`receiptAllocations`), the legacy payments stored in `payments`, and the credit-note applications (`creditApplications`). That is the same arithmetic `src/invoice-engine.js` `outstanding()` performs, narrowed to the As-of date so the position is the one that existed on the report date rather than today's. A receipt is never spread across invoices by this module - only the allocation records are read - so a receipt nobody applied stays identifiable as a Customer Advance or an Unallocated receipt instead of quietly paying down an invoice. Outstanding is floored at zero, so a customer never shows a negative balance; money paid ahead of an invoice is stated beside the invoices, not as negative debt.

Aging is counted in DAYS PAST DUE from each invoice's own due date, never from its invoice date: `daysPastDue(dueDate,asOf)` and `agingBucket(days)` place the invoice in the fixed `AGING_BUCKETS` vocabulary - Not Yet Due, Due Today, 1–30 Days Overdue, 31–60 Days Overdue, 61–90 Days Overdue, 90+ Days Overdue. The ranges are exhaustive and non-overlapping by construction - a null or negative day count is Not Yet Due, `0` is Due Today, then `1–30`, `31–60`, `61–90` and `90+` - so no day is counted twice or missed and an invoice is never aged before it is payable. An invoice due today has a bucket of its own instead of being folded into the not-yet-due total. `invoiceStatus` states Paid when nothing is owed, then follows the due date - Overdue past the due date, Due Today on it, Partially Paid when money has been applied but the invoice is not yet due, and Not Yet Due otherwise; the `INVOICE_STATUSES` vocabulary the Status filter offers is exactly the set `invoiceStatus` can return. Paid invoices are not shown by default: with the Status filter on All, `inStatus` keeps only the rows that still owe money, and a paid invoice is reached by choosing Status = Paid, where it is stated with an outstanding of zero.

Only posted documents are read. A draft invoice, a draft receipt, a draft credit note, a cancelled invoice and an unposted journal contribute nothing, and a reversal is an ordinary posted entry, so a reversed pair nets to zero by itself. Customer, Status and Search narrow which invoices are stated, so each customer's own rows always sum to the figure on that customer's line. The Aging filter is deliberately different: choosing a bucket selects the CUSTOMERS that have money in that bucket (`inAging`) and does not narrow their rows, because the Outstanding column states the customer's whole balance and a bucket is not that balance. The bucket's own amount travels with the row as `entry.agingAmount` (`aging === All` is `0`, meaning "no hint") and the page prints it under Outstanding as a hint such as `Not Yet Due: ₹7,080.00`, so the filtered figure and the total can never be mistaken for one another. Search reads the customer master's name, code, phone and email, so the report uses the existing customer records rather than a copy of them.

The reconciliation compares the whole scoped subledger with the Accounts Receivable control account - the accounts the Chart of Accounts already marks as control accounts whose name reads Receivable or Debtor, plus the receivable account the accounting configuration names (`src/customer-outstanding.js` names no code of its own). The invoice subledger nets off only what was applied to an invoice, while the control account already nets off the whole receipt and the whole credit note, so the difference between the two is exactly the money held but not yet applied - an unallocated receipt or an unapplied credit note - and `report.reconciliation` states `control`, `subledgerOutstanding`, `unallocatedReceipts`, `unappliedCredit`, `expected` and `difference`. A genuine mismatch (a receivable posting that belongs to no invoice, receipt or credit note) is stated, never forced away. Advances posted to a liability sit outside the control account entirely, which is why they are reported beside it. The banner prints the difference as an absolute amount and carries a state class - `balanced`, `mismatch` or `unknown` - so the reader sees a green `Reconciles` with `Difference: ₹0.00`, an orange `Does not reconcile` with the real figure, or `No Accounts Receivable control account is configured`.

`report.checks` re-derives ten invariants - the customer totals against the invoice rows, the aging buckets against the outstanding, the status split, the row arithmetic, that nothing is negative, that every bucket really is the invoice's days past due, that paid rows stay hidden unless asked for, that no allocation is counted twice, and that the reconciliation holds - and `report.valid` is their conjunction. The page states the result instead of assuming the arithmetic held.

The page reuses the report shell already established by the Balance Sheet, the Trial Balance, the Profit & Loss and the Cash Flow Statement: one white report surface, a quiet heading with the title and the description "See what your customers currently owe you.", and a toolbar that carries nothing but the inline customer search, an anchored Filters popover, Export Excel and Export PDF. Four summary cards state Total Receivable, Overdue, Due Today and Not Yet Due. The table is exactly SEVEN columns - Customer, Customer ID, Invoices, Outstanding, Overdue, Not Yet Due and Actions - and permanent aging columns are gone by design: aging is reachable only through the filter, so a business owner is never handed four bucket columns to read. The caption states the position (`Customer receivable position as of 2026-09-28`). Filters own As of Date, Branch, Status, Aging and Customer, with every option derived from the loaded records rather than hard-coded, plus Refresh and Clear filters; the search is applied through `useDeferredValue` so the page reports `aria-busy` while recalculating. The reconciliation banner is a sticky footer. Clicking a customer expands a detail card stating Customer, Customer ID, Total outstanding, Overdue and Not yet due - plus Customer advance, Unallocated receipt and Unapplied credit note, each only when it is non-zero - above a NINE-column invoice table, Invoice, Invoice Date, Due Date, Invoice Amount, Paid, Credit Applied, Outstanding, Status and Actions, whose Status cell appends `Days overdue: N` only when the invoice really is past due. The empty state reads "No outstanding customer balances found." above "Try changing the date or filters." with a Reset filters action.

Every action hands off to the workflow that owns the document instead of touching accounting, and no row is ever actionable only through a clipped button: the actions live in the shared menu `src/OutstandingActions.jsx` (the same `soMoreButton` / `soActionMenu` control the invoices, sales orders and credit notes registers open), so a narrow table can never push an action off-screen. A customer row's menu offers View Customer, View Statement, View Invoices and Record Receipt; an invoice row's menu offers Open Invoice and Record Payment, and adds View Payment or View Credit Note only when a receipt or a credit note is actually attached to that invoice. View Statement stores `sessionStorage['wayvida-credit-customer']` and opens the existing Customer Statement, View Customer and View Invoices open their registers, Record Receipt and Record Payment store `sessionStorage['wayvida-receive-invoice']` and open Payments Received, Open Invoice stores `sessionStorage['wayvida-open-invoice']` and opens Invoices, View Payment stores `sessionStorage['wayvida-open-receipt']`, and View Credit Note stores `sessionStorage['wayvida-open-credit']`. The page reads the accounting state and the customer master and never writes either.

Three shell rules bite here and were only visible in the running app: the report heading is a `<header>` element, so `.coHead` restates `position:static!important;inset:auto!important;z-index:auto!important;height:auto!important` exactly as `.bsHead`, `.tbHead`, `.plHead` and `.cfHead` do, or the bare `header{position:fixed;...}` in `src/styles.css` lifts it out of the flow and over the summary cards; `th:last-child,td:last-child{...text-align:right}` from `src/ui-system.css` right-aligns the customer cell, which holds the expand button, unless `.coStatementCard .coCustomer th` restates `text-align:left!important`; and the report must fit its container rather than the page, so the seven columns are sized in percent through `.coNameCol` / `.coCodeCol` / `.coCountCol` / `.coOutCol` / `.coDueCol` / `.coActionsCol` and the table is measured to fit `.coTableScroll` at the standard content width. Below 560px the phone rules hide the Customer ID column and the row's View button (`.coCodeCol` and `.coViewButton`), because the More menu already carries every action and the drill-down is reachable from the customer name; the phone rules are scoped as `.coStatementCard>.coTableScroll>table{table-layout:fixed}` rather than a bare `.coStatementCard table`, because the bare form also matched the nine-column invoice table nested in the drill-down and forced a fixed layout on it inside a phone-width wrapper, collapsing every column and letting the nowrap text collide. The invoice table cannot fold, so it is a separate case: `.coInvoiceScroll` gives it its own horizontal scroll and the table carries `min-width:760px`, keeping its columns at content width instead of squeezing them. The customer name uses `overflow-wrap:anywhere`, so a long name wraps inside its cell instead of widening the table.

The App shell renders both portal reports through `createPortal` from `src/Navigation.jsx`, which appends them to `document.body` rather than into `<main>`. `src/App.jsx` therefore excludes `Customer Outstanding` and `Supplier Outstanding` from the generic "is ready" fallback card (and from the `welcome` block) with `'Customer Outstanding'===a||'Supplier Outstanding'===a?null:...`, so `<main>` stays empty and the redundant page-name heading and its "Create invoice" button cannot appear in the accessibility tree, in print or in a page-height calculation. The other legacy portal reports (Balance Sheet, Trial Balance, Profit & Loss, Cash Flow Statement, Transaction Register, Audit Log) still fall through to that card; it is covered by the fixed portal overlay on screen but does print above them.

`tests/customer-outstanding.test.mjs` covers the per-invoice arithmetic including the worked credit-note example, the aging buckets computed from the due date, every status, the paid-invoice behaviour through the Status filter, the draft, cancelled and unposted exclusions, the advance and unallocated receipt, every filter including search by name, code, phone and email, that the Aging filter selects customers without narrowing their rows and reports the bucket amount as the hint, the reconciliation against the control account in both the matching and the mismatched case, the ten checks, the immutable source, the simplified export header and totals, the filtered export, and the page, sidebar and reports-grid contract (seven columns, no `coBucketCol`, no `includePaid`, the shared `OutstandingActions` menu, and the scoped phone rule).

Two properties of the seeded demo data are worth recording because they look like report bugs and are not. The demo's `demo-banking:receipt` journal credits Accounts Receivable by Rs 50,000 with no receipt document behind it, so the reconciliation surfaces a Rs 50,000 difference at the default As-of date instead of absorbing it; and the only customer invoice in the demo (INV-00002, Northstar Services, Rs 2,950.00) is not yet due, which is why an As-of date near the report date shows a receivable with an Overdue of zero.
