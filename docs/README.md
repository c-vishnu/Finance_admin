**Cash & Banking checkpoint:** the `Reports` section now carries a `Cash & Banking` group with exactly two leaves - `Cash & Bank Book` and `Bank Reconciliation` (route name `Bank Reconciliation Report`) - implemented by `src/CashAndBankBook.jsx` and `src/BankReconciliationReport.jsx` over ONE pure projection module `src/cash-banking.js` and ONE shared sheet `src/cash-banking.css`, both mounted through `createPortal` from `src/Navigation.jsx` into the shared `.transactionPortal` (so `src/App.jsx` excludes the two route names from the operational-module switch and the welcome banner). The Cash & Bank Book is a transaction-level book, not a statement: one row per POSTED journal line that touched an account the Chart of Accounts already classifies as money, in date order, with the ledger's own running balance when one account is selected. The eligible accounts come from the shared `isMoneyAccount` rule (`cash|bank|wallet` in name, group, nature or purpose, ACTIVE, non-group, `Assets`), so no account code is hard-coded and every report's definition of money stays single-sourced; a ledger merely named after a bank with no such wording and no group, an inactive ledger, a group header and a ledger named `Bank Charges` but typed `Expenses` are all excluded. Date From, Date To, Account and Branch decide the book, so they move the opening balance, the closing balance and every account total; Transaction Type, Reconciliation Status and Search narrow only the rows listed. `opening + receipts - payments === closing === ledgerClosing` holds by construction, the opening balance is the ledger balance before Date From rather than the sum of the rows, `difference = ledgerClosing - closing` is stated and never forced away, and six `checks` re-derive every figure. A transfer between two of the business's own money accounts is marked `internal` on both sides and contributes zero to the combined position. Across every account no combined running balance is invented - each account's own opening, receipts, payments and closing balance is stated in the `Account balances` block instead. A reconciliation word is never guessed: `Reconciled` comes from the Banking module's own `bankMatches` row, `Excluded` from a same-account same-reference excluded statement line, `Unreconciled` from an eligible account with no match, and a cash account stays `Posted`. The Bank Reconciliation report is a control report over the same banking records: it reuses `reconciliationSummary` for the book balance, statement balance, reconciled amount, unreconciled amount and difference, merges a matched pair into one row and leaves the missing side BLANK rather than zero, states `Not available` and `No bank statement has been imported for this period` when no statement line falls in the period instead of inventing a balance, and offers `Match` / `View Statement Line` / `View Transaction` only as handoffs into the Banking workspace and the General Ledger, because a match is created there and never here. Reconciling a statement line never moves a ledger balance. Read `docs/CASH-AND-BANKING.md` before changing either page or the projection.

**Transaction Register checkpoint:** the Transaction Register is a read-only DOCUMENT INDEX at the top of the Reports section, implemented by `src/transaction-register.js` and `src/TransactionRegister.jsx`. It is not a second Day Book, General Ledger, Journal register or Cash Book: it answers which business transactions and documents exist and where each one is opened, so a user can find, review and open any business transaction in one place. The projection joins the four stores a transaction can live in - `readAccounts()` (`src/account-store.js`), `readOperations()` (`src/operations-store.js`), `BANKING_KEY` (`wayvida-banking-v1`) and `MANUAL_JOURNAL_KEY` (`wayvida-manual-journals-v2`) - and states nine types (Sales Invoice, Credit Note, Customer Receipt, Purchase Bill, Debit Note, Supplier Payment, Expense, Journal, Bank Transaction) as ONE row each; sales and purchase orders are commitments, never transactions, and are deliberately absent. A document and the journal the engine posted for it are one transaction, so the journal is listed only when no document claims it, and a manual journal suppresses its own ledger entry through its own `ledgerJournalId` - one row per transaction is a guarantee, not an assumption. Every row is reduced to Draft, Pending, Posted, Reversed or Cancelled by its own module's posting rule, the Amount is the document total through `money()`, and the register prints NO debit, credit or accounting impact anywhere; that detail stays in the Day Book, the General Ledger and the Journal register. The page reuses the Day Book / Customer Outstanding shell and adds nothing new to the visual language: a 300px search beside one Filters disclosure (Date from, Date to, Transaction type, Status, Branch, Customer, Supplier plus the order control - every option derived from the rows themselves), Export Excel and Export PDF that state the scope and the applied filters, four filter-driven summary cards (Total Transactions, Posted, Draft, Cancelled) that always add up to the total, an eight-column register, the shared `StatusPill` and `EmptyState`, an alert with Retry, and a table-only horizontal scroll that drops the Reference column at 760px and the Customer / Supplier column at 560px. It is PAGED rather than one long table: twenty-five rows by default, 25/50/100 through the shared `.pagination-footer` pager reading `Showing 1-25 of 113 transactions` / `N per page` / `Page 1 of 3`, and no grand total, because an invoice, a receipt and an expense do not add up. Rows off the current page are hidden with `.trOffPage` and released in print, so the PDF still states the whole filtered dataset. `SORT_OPTIONS` orders by Newest first (the default), Oldest first, Highest amount, Lowest amount or Document number through `sortTransactions`, which copies the list and falls back to the document number so two documents dated the same day keep one stable order; searching, filtering, re-sorting and changing the page size all return to page one and reset nothing else, so a search never clears a filter. Dates render through the shared `toLocaleDateString('en-IN',{day:'2-digit',month:'short',year:'numeric'})` expression while the projection keeps ISO dates so the filters stay string-comparable, the Document No. is a link-styled `button.trLink`, and the party and reference cells truncate with an ellipsis while keeping the full value on `title`. View opens the source document through the existing sessionStorage handshakes; the handed-off value is `handoffId`, which is the bank ACCOUNT for a statement line because the Banking feed is selected by account, and a supplier payment opens the Payments Made register with no handshake because it has no document page of its own. Read `docs/TRANSACTION-REGISTER.md` before changing it.

**Customer Outstanding checkpoint:** the Customer Outstanding is a read-only, date-based receivables subledger at its own Reports leaf, implemented by `src/customer-outstanding.js` and `src/CustomerOutstanding.jsx`. Outstanding is the posted invoice total less the receipt allocations, legacy payments and credit-note applications actually applied to that invoice, read as at the As-of date; a credit sale is never a receivable until it is posted and a receipt is never spread across invoices on its own, so an unallocated or advance receipt stays identifiable as exactly that. Aging is counted from each invoice's own due date in days past due, never from its invoice date, and outstanding is floored at zero so a customer never shows negative debt. The report reconciles the whole scoped subledger with the Accounts Receivable control account and states the difference instead of forcing it away, with ten checks re-deriving every figure. Since 2026-09-28 the layout is deliberately minimal and identical to the Supplier Outstanding apart from terminology: a seven-column table (Customer, Customer ID, Invoices, Outstanding, Overdue, Not Yet Due, Actions), four summary cards, and aging only as a FILTER over Not Yet Due / Due Today / 1–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / 90+ Days Overdue. There are no permanent aging columns, and choosing a bucket selects the customers holding money in it while the Outstanding column still states the whole balance, with the bucket amount shown beneath as a hint. Do not reintroduce bucket columns, the Include paid toggle (paid documents are reached by setting Status to Paid) or a per-bucket summary card. Read `docs/CUSTOMER-OUTSTANDING.md` before changing it.

**Supplier Outstanding checkpoint:** the Supplier Outstanding is a read-only, date-based payables subledger at its own Reports leaf, implemented by `src/supplier-outstanding.js` and `src/SupplierOutstanding.jsx`, and it is the SAME design as the Customer Outstanding - only the terminology differs (supplier, bill, payable, payment, debit note). Outstanding is the posted purchase bill total less the vendor payments and debit-note applications actually applied to that bill, as of the selected date, so it is never total bills minus total payments; a payment names exactly one bill, a payment with no bill behind it stays a Supplier Advance or Unallocated payment, and outstanding is floored at zero. Aging is days past due from the bill's own due date, paid bills are hidden until the Status filter asks for them, drafts, cancellations, voided debit notes, reversed payments and unposted journals contribute nothing, and the whole scoped subledger is reconciled against the Accounts Payable control account - derived from the Chart of Accounts, never a hard-coded code - with the difference stated rather than forced away. The seven-column table, the four summary cards, the aging-as-filter rule and the shared `src/OutstandingActions.jsx` row menu are the Customer Outstanding's, and `tests/supplier-outstanding.test.mjs` pins that parity. `src/Vendors.jsx` consumes `sessionStorage['wayvida-open-vendor']` so View Statement reuses the existing vendor ledger. The class prefix is `spo*`, not `so*`, because `src/sales-orders.css` already owns `so*`.

**Cash Flow Statement checkpoint:** the Cash Flow Statement is a read-only, period-based statement of the cash and cash-equivalent movements that actually happened, at its own Reports leaf, implemented by `src/cash-flow.js` and `src/CashFlowStatement.jsx`. It is never `revenue - expenses`: it reads only the posted ledger, so a credit sale or credit purchase creates no cash flow until it is settled. Cash and cash equivalents are the accounts the Chart of Accounts already classifies as money (`isMoneyAccount`, re-exported as `isCashEquivalent`), and each cash leg is classified by the other side of its own entry into the fixed Operating / Investing / Financing vocabulary of `ACTIVITY_SECTIONS`. A journal that only moves money between the business's own cash and bank accounts is stated once on the derived `internalTransfers` row and never invented as an inflow and an outflow, and the movements the filters leave out are stated on the `excluded` row, so `openingCash + operating + investing + financing + internalTransfers + excluded === closingCash` holds by construction. `difference` is reported and never forced to zero, the ten `report.checks` re-derive every total, `report.statement` is the one ordered list the page and `cashFlowExportRows` both walk, and the page reuses the report shell with five summary cards, an anchored Filters popover and Export Excel / Export PDF. See `docs/CASH-FLOW-STATEMENT.md`.

**Profit & Loss checkpoint:** the Profit & Loss is a read-only, period-based posted-journal income statement at its own Reports leaf, implemented by `src/profit-loss.js` and `src/ProfitLoss.jsx`. Read `docs/PROFIT-AND-LOSS.md` before changing it; a subtotal must keep its label and amount in one full-width cell or the phone layout prints totals without their amounts.
