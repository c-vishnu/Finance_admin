# Supplier Outstanding

`src/SupplierOutstanding.jsx` is a read-only, date-based payables subledger and `src/supplier-outstanding.js` is its pure projection. `supplierOutstanding(state,vendors,{asOf,branch,supplier,status,aging,search})` reads the posted accounting records and answers one question - how much do we currently owe each supplier as of the selected date. It is a subledger, not a second General Ledger: it never posts, edits or reverses anything, and it never re-derives a payable from a UI document while posted accounting data exists.

Outstanding is the purchase bill's own total less the money actually applied to that bill: the vendor payments recorded against it (`vendorPayments` rows whose `billId` is that bill) and the debit notes actually applied to it (`debitNotes` rows in status `Adjusted`, taken at `appliedAmount`). That is the same arithmetic `src/purchase-service.js` `vendorOutstanding()` performs, narrowed to the As-of date so the position is the one that existed on the report date rather than today's. It is never total bills minus total payments: a vendor payment names exactly one bill, so only that bill is reduced, and a payment with no bill behind it stays identifiable as a Supplier Advance or an Unallocated payment instead of quietly paying down a bill nobody applied it to. Outstanding is floored at zero, so a supplier never shows a negative balance; money paid ahead of a bill is stated beside the bills, not as negative debt.

Aging is counted in DAYS PAST DUE from each bill's own due date, never from its bill date: `daysPastDue(dueDate,asOf)` and `agingBucket(days)` place the bill in the fixed `AGING_BUCKETS` vocabulary - Not Yet Due, Due Today, 1–30 Days Overdue, 31–60 Days Overdue, 61–90 Days Overdue, 90+ Days Overdue. The ranges are exhaustive and non-overlapping by construction - a null or negative day count is Not Yet Due, `0` is Due Today, then `1–30`, `31–60`, `61–90` and `90+` - so no day is counted twice or missed and a bill is never aged before it is payable. A bill due today has a bucket of its own instead of being folded into the not-yet-due total. `billStatus` states Paid when nothing is owed, then follows the due date - Overdue past the due date, Due Today on it, Partially Paid when money has been applied but the bill is not yet due, and Not Yet Due otherwise; the `BILL_STATUSES` vocabulary the Status filter offers is exactly the set `billStatus` can return. Paid bills are not shown by default: with the Status filter on All, `inStatus` keeps only the rows that still owe money, and a paid bill is reached by choosing Status = Paid, where it is stated with an outstanding of zero.

Only posted documents are read. A draft purchase bill, a draft (or submitted, approved or cancelled) debit note, a reversed vendor payment, a cancelled bill and an unposted journal contribute nothing, and a reversal is an ordinary posted entry, so a reversed pair nets to zero by itself. Supplier, Status and Search narrow which bills are stated, so each supplier's own rows always sum to the figure on that supplier's line. The Aging filter is deliberately different: choosing a bucket selects the SUPPLIERS that have money in that bucket (`inAging`) and does not narrow their rows, because the Outstanding column states the supplier's whole balance and a bucket is not that balance. The bucket's own amount travels with the row as `entry.agingAmount` (`aging === All` is `0`, meaning "no hint") and the page prints it under Outstanding as a hint such as `Not Yet Due: ₹7,080.00`, so the filtered figure and the total can never be mistaken for one another. Search reads the vendor master's name, code, phone and email, so the report uses the existing vendor records rather than a copy of them.

The reconciliation compares the whole scoped subledger with the Accounts Payable control account. `payableAccounts(books)` derives that account from the Chart of Accounts rather than naming a code: an account counts when it reads as a payable or creditor ledger AND is flagged as a control account or carries the payables account nature (`Accounts Payable` / `Supplier Payable`), plus the account the accounting configuration names in `config.ap`. That is what keeps `GST Payable` out of the payables control even though its name contains the word - `src/supplier-outstanding.js` names no code of its own. The control balance is stated as a payable (credits less debits) so a positive figure is money owed, and it is scoped by branch exactly the way the Trial Balance scopes its movement. The bill subledger nets off only what was applied to a bill, while the control account already nets off the whole payment and the whole debit note, so the difference between the two is exactly the money held but not yet applied - `report.reconciliation` states `control`, `subledgerOutstanding`, `unallocatedPayments`, `unappliedDebitNotes`, `expected` and `difference`. A genuine mismatch (a payable posting that belongs to no bill, payment or debit note) is stated, never forced away. The banner prints the difference as an absolute amount and carries a state class - `balanced`, `mismatch` or `unknown` - so the reader sees a green `Reconciles` with `Difference: ₹0.00`, an orange `Does not reconcile` with the real figure, or `No Accounts Payable control account is configured`.

`report.checks` re-derives ten invariants - the supplier totals against the bill rows, the aging buckets against the outstanding, the status split, the row arithmetic, that nothing is negative, that every bucket really is the bill's days past due, that paid rows stay hidden unless asked for, that no allocation is counted twice, and that the reconciliation holds - and `report.valid` is their conjunction. The page states the result instead of assuming the arithmetic held.

The page reuses the report shell already established by the Balance Sheet, the Trial Balance, the Profit & Loss, the Cash Flow Statement and the Customer Outstanding: one white report surface, a quiet heading with the title and the description "See what you currently owe your suppliers.", and a toolbar that carries nothing but the inline supplier search, an anchored Filters popover, Export Excel and Export PDF. Four summary cards state Total Payable, Overdue, Due Today and Not Yet Due. The table is exactly SEVEN columns - Supplier, Supplier ID, Bills, Outstanding, Overdue, Not Yet Due and Actions - and permanent aging columns are gone by design: aging is reachable only through the filter, so a business owner is never handed four bucket columns to read. The caption states the position (`Supplier payable position as of 2026-09-04`). Filters own As of Date, Branch, Status, Aging and Supplier, with every option derived from the loaded records rather than hard-coded, plus Refresh and Clear filters; the search is applied through `useDeferredValue` so the page reports `aria-busy` while recalculating. The reconciliation banner is a sticky footer. Clicking a supplier expands a detail card stating Supplier, Supplier ID, Total payable, Overdue and Not yet due - plus Supplier advance, Unallocated payment and Unapplied debit note, each only when it is non-zero - above a NINE-column bill table, Bill, Bill Date, Due Date, Bill Amount, Paid, Debit Note / Return Applied, Outstanding, Status and Actions, whose Status cell appends `Days overdue: N` only when the bill really is past due. The empty state reads "No outstanding supplier balances found." above "Try changing the date or filters." with a Reset filters action.

Every action hands off to the workflow that owns the document instead of touching accounting, and no row is ever actionable only through a clipped button: the actions live in the shared menu `src/OutstandingActions.jsx` (the same `soMoreButton` / `soActionMenu` control the invoices, sales orders and credit notes registers open), so a narrow table can never push an action off-screen. A supplier row's menu offers View Supplier, View Statement, View Bills and Record Payment; a bill row's menu offers Open Bill and Record Payment, and adds View Payment or View Debit Note / Return only when a payment or a debit note is actually attached to that bill. View Statement stores `sessionStorage['wayvida-open-vendor']` and opens the existing vendor ledger (the Vendors register's Transactions tab for that supplier - `src/Vendors.jsx` consumes the key once) because there is no separate Vendor Statement screen to reuse; View Supplier and View Bills open their registers, Record Payment stores `sessionStorage['wayvida-pay-bill']` and opens Payments Made, Open Bill stores `sessionStorage['wayvida-open-bill']` and opens Purchase Bills, and the per-bill View Payment and View Debit Note actions store `sessionStorage['wayvida-open-vendor-payment']` and `sessionStorage['wayvida-open-debit-note']`. The page reads the accounting state and the vendor master and never writes either.

Three shell rules bite here and are only visible in the running app: the report heading is a `<header>` element, so `.spoHead` restates `position:static!important;inset:auto!important;z-index:auto!important;height:auto!important` exactly as `.bsHead`, `.tbHead`, `.plHead`, `.cfHead` and `.coHead` do, or the bare `header{position:fixed;...}` in `src/styles.css` lifts it out of the flow and over the summary cards; `th:last-child,td:last-child{...text-align:right}` from `src/ui-system.css` right-aligns the supplier cell, which holds the expand button, unless `.spoStatementCard .spoSupplier th` restates `text-align:left!important`; and the report must fit its container rather than the page, so the seven columns are sized in percent through `.spoNameCol` / `.spoCodeCol` / `.spoCountCol` / `.spoOutCol` / `.spoDueCol` / `.spoActionsCol` and the table is measured to fit `.spoTableScroll` at the standard content width. Below 560px the phone rules hide the Supplier ID column and the row's View button (`.spoCodeCol` and `.spoViewButton`), because the More menu already carries every action and the drill-down is reachable from the supplier name; the phone rules are scoped as `.spoStatementCard>.spoTableScroll>table{table-layout:fixed}` rather than a bare `.spoStatementCard table`, because the bare form also matched the nine-column bill table nested in the drill-down and forced a fixed layout on it inside a phone-width wrapper, collapsing every column and letting the nowrap text collide. The bill table cannot fold, so it is a separate case: `.spoBillScroll` gives it its own horizontal scroll and the table carries `min-width:760px`, keeping its columns at content width instead of squeezing them. The supplier name uses `overflow-wrap:anywhere`, so a long name wraps inside its cell instead of widening the table.

The class prefix is `spo*`, deliberately not `so*`: `src/sales-orders.css` already owns `so*` (including `soFiltersMore` and `soNotes`), so a second `so*` report would restyle the Sales Orders screens through CSS that is global to the bundle. The shared row menu keeps its `so*` class names for the opposite reason - it IS the register menu, so reusing `.soMoreButton` / `.soActionMenu` is what makes the report read as the rest of the product.

The App shell renders both portal reports through `createPortal` from `src/Navigation.jsx`, which appends them to `document.body` rather than into `<main>`. `src/App.jsx` therefore excludes `Supplier Outstanding` and `Customer Outstanding` from the generic "is ready" fallback card (and from the `welcome` block) with `'Customer Outstanding'===a||'Supplier Outstanding'===a?null:...`, so `<main>` stays empty and the redundant page-name heading and its "Create invoice" button cannot appear in the accessibility tree or in print. The other legacy portal reports (Balance Sheet, Trial Balance, Profit & Loss, Cash Flow Statement, Transaction Register, Audit Log) still fall through to that card; it is covered by the fixed portal overlay on screen but does print above them.

`tests/supplier-outstanding.test.mjs` covers the spec's worked payables cases - a 59,000 bill less a 30,000 payment leaves 29,000, the same bill less a further 9,000 debit note leaves 20,000, a 20,000 payment against a 50,000 bill leaves 30,000, and a 50,000 credit purchase with no payment is a 50,000 payable that settles to nil - plus the aging buckets computed from the due date, every status, the paid-bill behaviour through the Status filter, the draft, cancelled, voided and reversed exclusions, the supplier advance and unallocated payment, the as-of narrowing on both the documents and the journal, every filter including search by name, code, phone and email, that the Aging filter selects suppliers without narrowing their rows and reports the bucket amount as the hint, the control-account derivation, the reconciliation against that control in both the matching and the mismatched case, the ten checks, the immutable source, the simplified export header and totals, the filtered export, the page, sidebar, reports-grid and vendor-ledger-handoff contract, and the explicit "the two outstanding reports are one design, differing only in terminology" test that pairs the two pages, the two engines and the two stylesheets token for token.

Two properties of the seeded demo data are worth recording because they look like report bugs and are not. The demo's only purchase bill, BILL-00001 (Rs 7,080.00, due 2026-10-03), is settled in full by a later seeded payment, so at the default As-of date the report opens on its empty state and the bill is reachable by setting Status to Paid; moving the As-of date before that payment (for example 2026-09-04) states the bill as Rs 7,080.00 Not Yet Due. And the banking demo's `demo-banking:vendor-payment` journal debits Accounts Payable by Rs 25,000 with no `vendorPayments` document behind it, so the reconciliation surfaces a Rs 25,000 difference instead of absorbing it - the same class of finding the Customer Outstanding reports for the demo's sample customer-payment journal.
